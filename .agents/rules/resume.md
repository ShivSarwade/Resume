# Resume Rules

The following rules apply to all resume-related work.

## Core Philosophy
* `resume.tex` is the source of truth.
* Never invent experience, technologies, responsibilities, metrics, achievements, or projects.
* A JD describes what the company wants; it is NOT evidence that I possess those skills.

## JD Analysis
* Analyze the JD before changing the resume.
* Extract:
  * technologies
  * frameworks
  * tools
  * engineering practices
  * development disciplines
  * responsibilities
  * domain requirements
  * important keywords
  * seniority expectations
* Rank requirements by importance.
* Compare JD requirements against my actual resume.
* Classify requirements as:
  * Strong match
  * Partial match
  * Weakly represented
  * Missing but genuinely experienced
  * Missing and not experienced

## Matching & Content Updates
* Only add something if there is actual evidence that I have used or done it.
* Never add a technology just because it appears in the JD.
* Never turn something I studied into professional experience.
* Remove or deprioritize irrelevant skills when they take valuable resume space.
* Prioritize skills and experience relevant to the target JD.
* Preserve factual accuracy over ATS keyword matching.
* Avoid keyword stuffing.

## Resume Writing Style
* Write like a real engineer.
* Simple, direct, concise English.
* Do not sound AI-generated.
* Do not use unnecessary corporate language.
* Avoid generic phrases such as:
  * responsible for
  * worked on
  * passionate about
  * highly motivated
  * cutting-edge
  * leveraged synergies
  * robust and scalable unless technically justified
* Prefer action + work + technology/context + result/impact.
* Use STAR/CAR principles when useful.
* Do not force STAR into every bullet.
* Focus on what I built, implemented, improved, automated, integrated, or optimized.
* Use metrics only when they are real.
* Never manufacture impact numbers.
* Do not exaggerate ownership or seniority.

## LaTeX Rules
* Preserve the existing resume structure and styling unless there is a clear reason to change it.
* Keep the resume concise.
* Prefer one page.
* Do not make unrelated formatting changes.
* Ensure the final LaTeX compiles successfully.
